Tethertone turns your Android phone into a speaker for your Mac. Everything the Mac plays, from music and video to calls and games, comes out of the phone, over a USB cable or Wi-Fi.

<b>How it works</b>
<ul>
<li>Install Tethertone on the Mac (macOS 14 or later) and press Start.</li>
<li>Scan the pairing QR code with this app. That's the whole setup; the phone remembers the Mac and reconnects on its own.</li>
</ul>

<b>Features</b>
<ul>
<li>USB and Wi-Fi at once: the QR code lists every route, and the phone uses whichever answers first, so the cable wins when it's plugged in.</li>
<li>Lossless: uncompressed 16-bit stereo audio, no codec.</li>
<li>Stays in sync: a jitter buffer (20–250 ms, 90 ms by default) plus continuous clock-drift correction, so delay doesn't creep up over long sessions.</li>
<li>Plays with the screen off, with a Stop button in the notification.</li>
<li>Live diagnostics: buffer depth, underruns, lost packets and drift correction.</li>
</ul>

<b>Privacy</b>
No account, no ads, no analytics, no cloud. The app talks only to your own Mac. A random token in the QR code keeps other people on your network out. The audio itself is not encrypted, so use the USB cable or a VPN on networks you don't trust.

The camera is used only to scan the pairing code.

Tethertone is free and open source under the MIT License. It was previously called AudioBridge.
